exits (7)

  • “And remember, you also must always sell, no questions asked, if the loss exceeds your maximum permissible loss level, which, for most investors is 7–8 percent.” — Gil Morales & Chris Kacher, In the Trading Cockpit with the O'Neil Disciples, p. 352
  • “If the trade goes against you—cut your losses and run. Never argue with the crowd—simply use your judgment to decide when to join and when to leave.” — Alexander Elder, The New Trading for a Living, p. 56
  • “Cutting losses short is all about aborting losses, exits. Letting profits run is all about exits as well. The entire axiom is about exits.” — Van K. Tharp, Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom, p. 23
  • “In fact, the golden rule of trading says to: Cut your losses short and let your profits run.” — Van K. Tharp, Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom, p. 292
  • “The elements of good trading are: (1) cutting losses, (2) cutting losses, and (3) cutting losses. If you can follow these three rules, you may have a chance.” — Ed Seykota, The Market Wizards (interview by Jack D. Schwager), p. 80
  • “If you acquire the skill of being able to let your winners run, all your small losses and occasional mistakes will be taken care of, and your account will grow quite rapidly.” — @TmarketL
  • “Be a great trader not a perfect trader. Keep losses small. Take partial amounts off the table as you let your winners run. Trade, trim and trail. Wash, rinse and repeat” — @SunriseTrader

expectancy (5)

  • “The amateurs in most fields ask for forecasts, while professionals simply manage information and make decisions based on probabilities.” — Alexander Elder, The New Trading for a Living, p. 62
  • “A professional trader expects to be profitable by the end of the month or the quarter, but ask him whether he’ll make money on his next trade and he’ll honestly say he doesn’t know.” — Alexander Elder, The New Trading for a Living, p. 218
  • “Expectancy is the mean R multiple of your trading system.” — Van K. Tharp, Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom, p. 106
  • “No free lunch in the market. Every dollar of profit is paid for with risk management, screen time, emotional control, or a drawdown you had to sit through. If it looks easy, you're the liquidity.” — @1ChartMaster
  • “You don’t need to know the outcome—you need to understand the probability behind the setup.” — docmcgraw.substack

process (5)

  • “Applying conventional wisdom produces conventional returns, not superperformance.” — Mark Minervini, Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard, p. 23
  • “The better the trade, the less screen time is needed. The worse the trade, the more screen time you end up spending to watch it.” — @SJosephBurns
  • “Ignore and avoid the people who are always telling you about what they’re going to do. Spend your time with the people who do things and then show you after. It took me too long to learn this. But it” — @JC_ParetsX
  • “I had a bear bias heading into the news and it’s not like anything “Not bearish” happened there, so we’ll stick with the bear bias (I do not know what the news is at this point - I never do).” — holeyprofitnewsletter.substack
  • “The edge is not in being right every time, it’s in executing the same process over and over without needing immediate validation.” — Alex (primetrading.substack)

discipline (4)

  • “You don’t have to involve yourself in every market movement. In fact, you should not attempt to.” — Mark Minervini, Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard, p. 288
  • “It is best to wait for a better setup.” — Gil Morales & Chris Kacher, In the Trading Cockpit with the O'Neil Disciples, p. 174
  • “Don’t anticipate! Wait until the market gives you the clues, the signals, the hints, before you move.” — Richard Smitten, Jesse Livermore: World's Greatest Stock Trader, p. 310
  • “The trait that all winners have in common is a very high level of discipline.” — Alexander Elder, Come In To My Trading Room, p. 241
